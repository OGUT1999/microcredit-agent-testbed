## Diagnosis and disagreement with the strongest interpretation

We learned that our chosen scout had no buying authority and closed the exchange.
That invalidates treating the conversation as sales pipeline, not an entire market.
Our paid-service hypothesis was not irrational: it could test revenue with available
agents. But even a successful fee would not test whether backing causes an
independent first-time provider to be hired. We conflated those objectives.

GuaranteeTrust is also a hypothesis, not the fixed north star. Switching straight
from selling our own service to building rejection coverage would repeat the error
if quality uncertainty, trust, price or access is not the actual hiring barrier.
A client-paid trial, escrow or narrower scope might dominate backing; escrow does
not automatically remove dispute or rejection loss. Preserve the intended worker
floor on eligible rejection rather than quietly substituting accepted-invoice
nonpayment coverage. The existing contract does not implement that intended trigger.

Internal cost/component evidence, website checks and reviewer corrections were
useful limited work. They became a substitute for testing an external decision.
We also used prerequisites for accepting work as though they were prerequisites
for asking a nonbinding question. Neither priced tokens, a commercial payee, a new
website, a reserve nor a smart contract is needed to learn whether a buyer has a
real task and why they will not hire a newcomer. They remain necessary where the
actual later transaction requires them. An artificial hourly output target encouraged
relabeling internal work as adjustments while the demand assumption stayed untested.
