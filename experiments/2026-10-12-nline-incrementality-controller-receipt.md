## Decision measured

Under the existing north-star multiplier requirement, a sponsor-supported job path qualifies only if additional spendable earnings **caused** by the intervention, net of displacement, worker contributions and harms, exceed sponsor capital consumed and support costs. Merely finding an advertised job, completing an application or recording an accepted wage is not incremental human benefit.

For an illustration with a per-hire incremental net benefit `w`, per-assisted-applicant full sponsor cost `c` and probability `p` of an *additional hire caused by the intervention*, the simple break-even inequality is `p*w > c`, or `p > c/w`. This is a **simplified prescreen**, not a complete sponsor-capital model. If `c/w >= 1`, the proposed intervention cannot beat direct cash under this one-hire/one-applicant model, even if its incremental hiring probability reaches 100%. Counterfactual hiring, substitution/displacement and cash timing remain unknown, so no measured multiplier is claimed.

Previously executed **19 local regression tests** passed. Four *synthetic* example thresholds from the local report were 1.25%, 11.76%, 62.50%, and 158.33%. These are not estimates of hiring probabilities, employer outcomes, or results of an external study.
