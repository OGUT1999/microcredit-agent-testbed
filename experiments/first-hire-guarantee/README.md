## The route

An independent client who already buys entry-level online work hires a worker who has no completed
jobs. The client pays the wage. If the work fails the client's own acceptance, the sponsor refunds the
client up to the contract price. The sponsor pays only on failure, so each sponsor dollar is leveraged
by the client's wage.

Against the direct-cash benchmark this scores about $260 to $1,180 of gross value to the hired worker
per $100 of sponsor cash, at failure rates of 30% to 10%. Cash delivers $85 to $87 per $100. Those
figures are gross of displacement, and cash reaches poorer people
([../direct-cash-benchmark](../direct-cash-benchmark/README.md)). The deciding unknown is take-up: will
any independent client hire a newcomer because of the guarantee?

## Channel check (desk research, 2026-10-11)

- **No programme like this exists.** No programme found guarantees or subsidises a client's first
  hire of new freelancers. Existing programmes work on the worker side:
  - a government and UNICEF training pilot in Sierra Leone (Learn2Earn, December 2025), with trainees
    applying to live projects on platforms such as Upwork;
  - an Inter-American Development Bank training trial in El Salvador, which raised first contracts
    but not sustained success;
  - an income-sharing "virtual migration" programme in Bangladesh;
  - a registered trial giving mentoring and application credits to African professionals.
- **The project cannot be the channel on a marketplace.** Upwork's terms forbid a client and freelancer
  who met there from moving the relationship or payment off the platform (circumvention). Our
  guarantee would be a separate agreement between the sponsor and a client. Even so, the project has
  no legitimate way to approach a marketplace's clients directly.
- **So the channel is a placement programme** that already sends graduates to clients and can offer
  the guarantee to those clients itself.

## Costs and limits

- **Guarantee fund:** at most 6 refunds of up to $60 each, so the maximum exposure is $360. The
  expected cost is about $65 at an 18% failure rate. It is paid only on a failure the client documents
  against acceptance terms written in advance.
- **Who pays:** refunds come from the operator's own account, so no agent can trigger one.
- **No contact without a decision:** nothing about the worker is collected beyond what the programme
  chooses to share.
- **Measurement:**
  - clients offered the guarantee, and clients accepting it;
  - hires of workers with no completed jobs;
  - first-contract acceptance rate;
  - refunds paid;
  - follow-on contracts at eight weeks, reported as description only. The paid-first-job packet
    explains why six cases cannot test the effect.
