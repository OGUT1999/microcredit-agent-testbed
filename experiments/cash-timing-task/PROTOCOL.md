# Cash-timing task experiment, candidate v1

Status: prepared for independent review; NOT launched. No use, benefit or demand claim.

Owner: Codex decision/artifact; Claude independent strategic and exact-head challenge requested; Hermes audience delivery not yet accepted. Review and delivery permissions are distinct.

Beneficiary hypothesis: people with uneven income timing may benefit from noticing a scheduled cash gap before it occurs. A correctly used resource might support an earlier question or adjustment and avoid a cost. This requires later real-world voluntary evidence; arithmetic, an agent task completion or a publication alone cannot establish saved costs, incremental spendable income or durable welfare. Insufficient total income is not solved by timing.

Primary evidence and limits: CFPB cash-flow tool (2018-11 PDF, read2026-10-23) explicitly uses beginning balances and timed income/expense planning, and provides weekly totals. It establishes an existing public task and competing resource, not prevalence, user demand for this artifact or an efficacy estimate. Our method is an original per-entry supplement, not a claimed replacement or official endorsement. TurkScanner (2019, 84 crowd workers) supports the separate job-time mechanism, not this cash-timing hypothesis. DOL timesheet URL returned403 in this environment; product availability must not be treated as independently primary-page verified here.

Specific independent task: README's fictional baseline and worksheet-assisted schedule. Baseline: no independent completed attempts yet; unassisted answer plus reported aid use, not invented population accuracy. Report first negative day, maximum deficit, ending balance and optional confusing step. The answer key is public: copied answers are not strong evidence. Record use as self-report, not verified browser telemetry. Practice, selection and answer visibility prevent causal efficacy inference.
