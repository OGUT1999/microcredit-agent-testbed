## Provisional strategic choice

**Make cash through an established delivery system, or improvement of that system, the first option to assess. Treat an added livelihood service as a challenger that must demonstrate an advantage—not as the default package to build.** Do not develop a general microloan, training, AI-coaching or payment product from these average effects. The studies do not establish which current population we can reach, what it lacks, or whether we add value over existing provision.

This sharpens the earlier broad livelihood-support recommendation. A bundled intervention can work, but evidence that a bundle works against the existing baseline is not evidence that it beats giving the same extra resources as cash. The decision is about what must be compared before choosing a component, not a recommendation to transfer money now. The project's existing sponsor-benefit accounting remains intact; no published study ratio is substituted for it.

## What the evidence changes

The decision-relevant difference is between **a plausible mechanism and an identified comparison**. A grant can relax a resource constraint without creating an obligation to repay. A service might address an additional constraint, but the relevant test is its gain over what the same resources could otherwise provide. Neither mechanism should be prescribed from poverty status alone. A person unable to take on more work needs a welfare-relevant option; leaving their subgroup effect unknown is preferable to forcing an entrepreneurship theory onto them.

The prior broad bundle hypothesis is therefore narrowed to **cash-benchmarked, population-specific components on an existing platform**, with cash/existing provision the default comparator and no component currently selected for delivery. Existing delivery improvement remains a separate rival: if a preventable access cost or missed entitlement is the binding problem, repairing it may dominate introducing either new cash or a new service. This claim needs a concrete case and measurement; the closed cross-country tables did not supply one.

### Net resources, earnings and full cost

For a future comparison, keep separate ledgers:

1. **Transfer resources:** amount actually received and usable, minus access/cash-out costs and any displacement of other support. This is not earned income.
2. **Net earnings:** labor income plus business profit after inputs and paid labor, accounting for displaced work and reporting own/family hours separately. Revenue, assets, working hours and businesses opened do not substitute for this measure.
3. **Household welfare:** consumption adequacy, unmet essentials, distress, resilience and relevant nonmonetary outcomes. Do not sum them mechanically with income or asset changes and double-count benefits.
4. **Full incremental cost:** transfers, delivery, supervision, safeguarding, evaluation, participant time/travel/care burdens, losses and capital-lock cost where relevant. Keep donor and household perspectives visible. Historical dollars, purchasing-power dollars and current local costs cannot be pooled without an explicit conversion and common horizon.

These are measurement requirements, not claims that all cited studies supplied every term. No proxy wage, assumed persistence rate or hypothetical household gain is inserted to make a proposal pass the project benchmark. Null statistical contrasts do not prove equality; large percentage gains from low baselines do not establish adequate living standards.

## Smallest decision-changing test

**First, a bounded feasibility-and-cost check for one existing delivery setting—not another country ranking or generic invitation.** The decision object must identify one opt-in target population, a documented unmet constraint, the existing service, a specific proposed component, a cash comparator deliverable through the same platform, and credible full incremental costs. Public existing information can be used now; organization contact, participant data and commitments require their applicable scope. If these inputs cannot be established, there is no justified field design or budget request. This is not a claim that a personal invitation is always necessary.

**Conditional external comparison:** retain the existing service for everyone, then compare the proposed component with the same additional delivery budget offered as cash; retain a status-quo arm where ethical and necessary to estimate absolute gains. Measure net household resources/earnings and unmet essentials, participant burdens and harms at aligned seasonal horizons, with longer follow-up before claiming durable welfare. Randomization, subgroup definitions, minimum meaningful effect and sample size require a capable evaluator and local governance. A few convenience responses would test usability at most, not poverty impact. No sample size, spending cap or trial launch is invented here.

The smallest result that changes the provisional choice is **credible evidence that a specific component adds enough relevant benefit, after its incremental costs and burdens, to beat the cash/existing-service alternative for the stated population**. If cash access is itself unavailable or costly, that must be measured rather than assuming a frictionless comparator. If the component does not clear this bar, prefer the better existing option or no new project. This design does not authorize delivery.
