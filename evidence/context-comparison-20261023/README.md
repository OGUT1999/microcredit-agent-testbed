# Comparing local contexts for household livelihood research

Codex (AI), 23 October 2026. Desk research following the accepted [global poverty report](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-agent-testbed/blob/cd844994ff96205e7e587e4618beaa7df4abdc09/evidence/global-poverty-research-20261023/README.md). This comparison does not select a partner, authorize contact or launch a program.

## Decision and criteria

**Prioritize a bounded reconciliation of Rwanda's payment-delivery evidence, retain northern Bangladesh as the alternative focused on access and adequacy, and retain western Kenya as a strong cash comparator.** This is a ranking of the next research questions, not of countries' need or expected impact. None is ready for our implementation. The practical change is to investigate a specific service failure before designing another financial mechanism.

The [screening protocol](https://github.com/scottonchain/microcredit-agent-testbed/issues/17#issuecomment-6375624690) was posted at 22:02:24 UTC before new context searches. It prioritizes: dated evidence of deprivation or exclusion for the population; the existing service and outcome baseline; delivery readiness; and additionality over that service without us. It requires at least one relevant primary need source and one documented delivery mechanism for further research, not a launch. Unknowns receive no invented score. Sources below were retrieved on 23 October; their observation dates differ.

The three initial scopes were rural western Kenya, rural Rwanda and rural northern Bangladesh. A country's poverty rate cannot establish a particular district's exclusion, and the national poverty definitions cannot be compared as a common severity scale. Existing-service participation is neither effective coverage nor a causal welfare result. Numeric rules for any later field comparison must be set before its new outcome data, as the accepted report requires.

### Rwanda has a specific measurement question

Rwanda's statistical institute reports national poverty of 27.4% for 2023/24 under revised methods; its published rural estimate is 31.6%, available here through an indexed official report excerpt. The national overview was readable, but the full poverty-profile PDF timed out. These rates describe need, not exclusion from a named service. [NISR EICV7 overview](https://statistics.gov.rw/statistical-publications/poverty-and-social-protection/eicv7-main-indicators-report-202324); [poverty profile](https://statistics.gov.rw/sites/default/files/documents/2025-04/EICV7_Poverty%20Profile.pdf).

The separately accessible **EICV7 VUP thematic report** uses a beneficiary sampling frame, designed for national estimates by program component—not a sample of everyone excluded. Table 4.2 reports 15.1% receiving their latest Direct Support payment on time in 2023/24. It also reports regular monthly receipt and predominantly SACCO delivery. Phone ownership among Direct Support households is only about 48%; a smartphone-only repair would be poorly matched. These are descriptive survey findings, not impacts. [NISR report, methods and tables 3.9/4.2](https://microdata.statistics.gov.rw/index.php/catalog/121/download/1095).

LODA's **FY2024/25 administrative report**, table 26, reports average Direct Support timeliness of 97%, with district variation. Its stated due rule is payment no later than ten days into the following month. The different period, denominator, meaning of “on time,” and recorded payment stage prevent a direct subtraction from the survey percentage. Table 26 does not establish a rural-only failure rate. [LODA report, printed page 29](https://www.loda.gov.rw/fileadmin/user_upload/LODA/Publications/Reports/LODA-Annual_Activity_Report_2024.2025_Final_version_signed.pdf).

**Challenge:** selecting Rwanda for clear public data risks confusing an observable discrepancy with a repairable problem. **Better alternative:** test whether the discrepancy disappears when both sources describe the same period, entitlement date and household-access stage. If it does, avoid a new tool; if it does not, compare existing operational remedies before proposing software. A household could receive a transfer regularly but too late for an essential bill, yet neither source proves that our intervention would prevent that harm.

A parallel graduation program is also a weak default: BRAC announced a district-led Nyamagabe collaboration targeting 10,287 households in June 2025. This is an announced target, not verified present coverage or an invitation to participate. It is evidence to check duplication. [BRAC announcement](https://www.bracinternational.org/news/nyamagabe-district-signs-mou-with-brac-to-launch-district-led-graduation-programme/?form=donate&recurring=once).

### Bangladesh offers an access and adequacy alternative

The World Bank's 2025 assessment, largely using HIES 2022, reports that only half of the poorest consumption quintile received social assistance in 2022. This is not the share of all eligible Rangpur households excluded. The assessment also identifies Rangpur's poor and vulnerable populations as especially exposed to drought and heat risks. Those regional findings support investigating resilience and access; they do not identify a current applicant or service failure. [Assessment, executive summary and chapter IV](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099112525183511448/pdf/P501761-dfa72c79-9394-4291-83e1-ea462da8221f.pdf).

BRAC reports a substantial existing graduation operation; its 2025 annual-report announcement attributes roughly 312,000 families graduating to the preceding five years. That is the operator's outcome classification, not an independent causal estimate or a Rangpur coverage denominator. The baseline includes existing assistance and livelihood support, not “no services.” [BRAC annual-report announcement](https://www.brac.net/stay-informed/news/brac-publishes-its-2025-annual-report/).

**Challenge:** an application aid or registry tool cannot repair insufficient funding, unsuitable eligibility rules, climate exposure or absent work. **Better alternative:** compare the reason for exclusion with adequacy of existing support. A local aggregate table linking eligible population, actual receipt, delays, benefit adequacy and inability to access the service would separate funding from administrative barriers. If insufficient resources dominate, improving or expanding existing support is the stronger proposal. No private beneficiary data or eligibility decisions are authorized here.

### Kenya sets a demanding cash and service baseline

A published randomized study delivered cash to over 10,500 poor households across 653 rural Kenyan villages and found gains in consumption and assets, alongside spillovers. It establishes a strong historical comparator, not a 2026 list of unserved households or a partnership. [Egger and colleagues, Econometrica 2022](https://emiguel.econ.berkeley.edu/research/general-equilibrium-effects-of-cash-transfers-experimental-evidence-from-kenya/).

A government presentation documents economic-inclusion delivery in 2020–24 and identifies payment, staffing, contracting and information-system challenges. It also describes expansion and dedicated work on referrals. These are implementation observations and plans, not a causal evaluation or proof that our software is wanted. [State Department presentation hosted by the World Bank, slides 2–7](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/a1e81e17109a11d8c895988f83cb6e3d-0140022025/related/Implementing-the-Vision-Program-Delivery-at-Scale-and-Policy-Integration-Kenya.pdf).

The July 2026 project paper's results framework retains future targets through 2030; its beneficiary totals include direct and indirect reach. It must not be read as an achieved poverty reduction count. [KSEIP2 project paper](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099072226140038339/pdf/P504218-145cf3e6-5c74-46d0-9910-82eaea767de2.pdf). The KNBS 2022 county poverty PDF was located but could not be fully retrieved in this pass, so no unverified county rate is used.

**Challenge:** strong institutions and familiar research can draw us toward already-served populations. **Better alternative:** require a current county-level service map before choosing western Kenya, and compare any proposed added support with cash or the existing program at equal full cost. If a separate underserved population is documented elsewhere, it should outrank familiarity. Retrieval failure is an evidence limitation, not evidence of lower need.

## Two ranked research alternatives and their tests

**1. Existing-transfer delivery reliability in Rwanda.** Owner: Codex for desk research; no local delivery owner appointed. Hypothesis: a preventable delay between entitlement and usable receipt may cause avoidable household expense or deprivation. Baseline: existing Direct Support, including established payment and complaint processes. First test: reconcile the two published measurement definitions and periods, then seek only public aggregate evidence of a remaining gap. This pass executes the first source comparison. Observation window: this bounded desk pass; decision deadline: its close, with no participant-response clock. Kill the proposed repair if aligned definitions explain the gap, the issue is resolved, or a simpler operational/funding response dominates. Authority: public research only. Next action: verify original question wording and administrative denominator; any later nonpublic audit or organizational contact requires a specific scope and permission. A prospective external comparison would measure usable receipt delay, access cost, unmet essentials and harms, not log entries or message counts.

**2. Assistance access and adequacy in northern Bangladesh.** Owner: Codex for desk research; no partner or recipient presumed. Hypothesis: a locally documented exclusion mechanism may prevent poor households from receiving useful existing support. Baseline: current assistance, BRAC or other existing services, and a comparable cash option. First test: a public district/upazila coverage-and-eligibility crosswalk that distinguishes lack of allocation from access failure. Baseline evidence currently remains national or divisional. Window: one further bounded evidence pass only if a concrete public local dataset is available; proposed decision deadline 24 October 22:00 UTC, not another agent's commitment or a schedule. Stop local selection if no aligned denominator can be established; do not repeat generic searching indefinitely. Authority: public research only. Next action: locate the published local aggregate table, not recruit or request personal records. A later external test would measure additional net resources and fewer unmet essentials against existing service, with predefined costs, harms and follow-up.

Kenya remains a research comparator, not a third ready experiment. The first two alternatives address different failures—timing of an existing entitlement versus exclusion or inadequacy—and neither assumes a loan is necessary. Preserving a household's time or avoiding fees could increase spendable resources, but those gains require measurement. Cash is not interchangeable with a loan or a token balance.
