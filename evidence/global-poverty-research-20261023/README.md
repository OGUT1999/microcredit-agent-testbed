# From financial mechanisms to dependable household livelihoods

**Global poverty research and strategic recommendation — 23 October 2026**
Prepared by Codex (AI). Research, not an offer, financial commitment, eligibility assessment or pilot authorization. Sources were checked on 23 October 2026; their measurement dates differ. This report responds to the requested higher-level research reset. It supersedes the unexecuted broadband/Prolific proposal, while preserving its history and existing unrelated observation windows.

## Executive recommendation

The project should become an **evidence-led household livelihood program supported by technology**, if it can secure competent local delivery. It should not begin by inventing another loan, token, marketplace or online invitation. The unit of success is a person's additional spendable resources and sustained wellbeing, after costs and risks—not a transaction or an account.

My recommended architecture is **stability first, then a first reliable earning opportunity**. Use existing cash assistance and services as the floor; help a household overcome a verified obstacle to an actual earning opportunity; pay fairly for useful work and preserve evidence of performance; add savings and practical support; consider credit only when an established cash flow can service it. Use ordinary local payment methods by default. This is an untested integration of established approaches, not a claim to have discovered a cure for poverty.

Three conclusions determine the recommendation:

1. Poverty is substantially a shortage of resources, dependable work, services and protection against shocks. A better payment interface cannot substitute for these.
2. Cash transfers and carefully delivered livelihood packages provide stronger general starting evidence than indiscriminate microcredit. That does not make grants universally sufficient or loans universally harmful.
3. Our clearest possible contribution is to make an existing delivery organization more effective at reaching excluded households and converting support into verified outcomes. We have not yet established that such an organization needs us, that participants are reachable, or that we can outperform its current practice.

The next strategic phase should therefore be a **comparative local implementation study**, not another public reception test. Select one population and delivery context, map what already serves it, and establish whether a specific failure in delivery or access is both important and repairable. The proposed program must compete against cash alone and existing services. If those baselines win, support or improve them rather than defend our invention.

## 1. What problem are we measuring?

The World Bank's September 2026 vintage estimates **824.3 million people, 10.1% of humanity, below $3 per person per day in 2024**. That is the latest year with sufficient survey coverage; **9.8% for 2026 is a nowcast**, not a new worldwide household survey. The 2024 estimate was revised downward by 22.5 million relative to the March vintage, substantially because of new data. A statistical revision is not evidence that our interventions changed lives. Extreme poverty remains concentrated in Sub-Saharan Africa, with worsening conditions in the Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan grouping. [World Bank, September 2026 update](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/opendata/september-2026-global-poverty-update-from-the-world-bank--one-in).

The $3 line uses **2021 purchasing-power parity**: a statistical adjustment for different price levels. It is not three US dollars exchanged into local currency each day. The complementary $4.20 and $8.30 lines reflect higher reference standards; national poverty measures and local essential-expense budgets remain necessary for implementation. Crossing a very low global line does not establish a secure livelihood. [World Bank poverty-line explanation, June 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2025/06/05/june-2025-update-to-global-poverty-lines).

Money is only one dimension. The **2026 Multidimensional Poverty Index** identifies 1.2 billion people experiencing acute combined deprivations across 108 countries covering 6.3 billion people. It reports 617 million children and more than eight in ten affected people living rurally. These estimates combine surveys of different dates and measure health, schooling and living conditions. They overlap monetary poverty; the populations must not be added together. The report's web summary has an ambiguous regional percentage, so this report does not use that percentage. [UNDP/OPHI, October 2026 statistical update](https://hdr.undp.org/content/2026-global-multidimensional-poverty-index).

Conflict, displacement, illness, disability, care obligations, weak transport and unreliable infrastructure can prevent people from using otherwise sensible financial products. A World Bank analysis published in June 2025 estimated 421 million extremely poor people in conflict- or instability-affected economies that year. This is an older model vintage, not a component to combine mechanically with the September 2026 totals. It nevertheless demonstrates why secure local institutions and basic services are central constraints. [World Bank, June 2025 analysis](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/06/27/fragile-and-conflict-affected-situations-intertwined-crises-multiple-vulnerabilities).

**Vulnerability differs from low average income.** A household can earn enough over a year yet go without food between harvests, lose a job after a health shock, or sell productive assets to meet an urgent bill. A useful diagnosis must distinguish: too little income; irregular timing; necessary expenses; inaccessible services; lack of productive assets; lack of buyers; and inability to work. These are different problems, requiring different combinations of assistance. Annual averages and wallet balances conceal them.

Our measurement proposal is to track net household resources, days of unmet essentials, earnings stability, debt distress, productive assets, and participant-defined wellbeing. Record time and care burdens, exclusion and safety incidents. Children and adults unable to work must not be classified as failed entrepreneurs. Household totals also do not establish that women or other vulnerable members control the resources.

## 2. What existing evidence supports—and does not support

The following evidence map separates causal studies from coverage descriptions. Even a good randomized study establishes effects in its studied context, not a universal recipe.

| Tool or mechanism | Important evidence | Implication and comparison to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Unrestricted cash | A large randomized Kenyan transfer study found gains in consumption and assets, positive local spillovers and little inflation in that setting. Its estimated local multiplier was 2.5. | Cash is a serious baseline, not a concession to failure. A local economic multiplier is not each recipient's income return or a universal estimate. [Egger et al., Econometrica 2022](https://emiguel.econ.berkeley.edu/research/general-equilibrium-effects-of-cash-transfers-experimental-evidence-from-kenya/) |
| Bundled livelihood support | Six-country randomized evaluations tested productive assets, temporary consumption support, coaching, training, savings and related support. Many benefits persisted beyond the intervention, with variation across sites. | Test an adequately delivered package, not a thin digital imitation. [Banerjee et al., Science 2015](https://emerge.ucsd.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/banerjee-a-multifaceted-program-causes-lasting-progress-for-the-very-poor.pdf) |
| Cash plus practical and psychosocial support | In Niger, a four-arm randomized study among women already receiving cash transfers found gains from multifaceted support; psychosocial components were particularly cost-effective. | Constraints can include confidence, social relations and opportunity as well as capital. This does not establish that an AI coach can replace a local worker. [Bossuroy et al., Nature 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-04647-8) |
| Microcredit | A synthesis of seven randomized experiments found average effects unlikely to be transformative, with more heterogeneous and larger effects among experienced business owners. | Prefer loans for demonstrated productive uses and repayment capacity; compare grants, buyer prepayment and no loan. [Meager, AEJ Applied Economics 2019](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/app.20170299) |
| Business grants over time | Uganda's startup grants raised earnings initially, but earnings and consumption differences had dissipated after nine years as controls caught up; some assets and skilled-work effects persisted. | Do not infer permanent escape from poverty from a good first year. [Blattman, Fiala and Martinez, AER Insights 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aeri.20190224) |
| Savings groups | Randomized studies in Ghana, Malawi and Uganda found improvements in business outcomes and women's empowerment, without evidence of average consumption gains. | Useful financial organization is not automatically broad poverty reduction. [Karlan et al., PNAS 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1611520114) |
| First employment and credible references | A randomized online-labor experiment hired 952 inexperienced workers; hiring and more informative public evaluations improved later employment outcomes. | A first useful paid task can overcome an information barrier. Transfer to today's labor markets and poor offline workers needs testing. [Pallais, AER 2014](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.104.11.3565) |
| Accessible payment and saving infrastructure | Research on Kenya's mobile-money expansion estimated poverty and resilience gains, especially for women. This was not a token-airdrop trial. | Distribution infrastructure matters when coupled to usable cash-out, saving and social networks. [Suri and Jack, Science 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah5309) |

Cash need not increase labor earnings to improve welfare. The US OpenResearch trial reports higher household income including transfers, but modest reductions in employment and income excluding transfers. It demonstrates the importance of reporting both measures and respecting recipients' choices; it is not a direct forecast for rural African livelihood programs. [OpenResearch study and linked papers](https://www.openresearchlab.org/projects/unconditional-cash-study).

Coverage remains far short of need. The World Bank's **2025 social-protection report** identifies two billion people in low- and middle-income countries without adequate protection, including people receiving benefits too small to be sufficient. Its **2024 economic-inclusion survey** covers 405 programs in 88 countries reaching over 70 million people directly or indirectly. Neither figure is a count of people lifted permanently out of poverty; definitions and denominators differ. [Social protection 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/topic/socialprotection/publication/state-of-social-protection-2025-2-billion-person-challenge); [economic inclusion 2024](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/e7d7c0e7-7885-479f-86da-42d0f4f1b65e).

The constructive lesson is not to abandon finance. It is to choose finance after diagnosing the obstacle, and to fund the human delivery and services that make it useful.

## 3. Four organizations: different mechanisms and different evidence

### RMMFI: enterprise support with capital and a local community

**Identity and model.** Rocky Mountain MicroFinance Institute is a Colorado nonprofit supporting entrepreneurs facing barriers to business ownership. Its approach combines business development, coaching and community with access to capital. The capital page describes launch loans up to $3,500 at 5% interest with a 2.5% closing fee and 12–18-month terms; current individual eligibility and availability require confirmation, not inference from this report. Participation and capacity-building are part of the model. [RMMFI approach](https://rmmfi.org/how-it-works/); [capital](https://rmmfi.org/capital/).

**Dated scale and outcomes.** Its report for October 2024–September 2025 says it engaged 566 entrepreneurs through several kinds of activity, ran 28 cohorts across eight sites, and received 5,516 volunteer hours. It reports a 51% average household-income increase over three years. These are organizational reports, not a verified causal estimate: this review could not establish the income sample, attrition or an equivalent untreated comparison. “Engaged” is not “completed” or “lifted out of poverty.” The report lists approximately $2.74 million revenue and $2.86 million expenses; these totals do not establish the unit cost of a successful business or isolate loan capital. Direct retrieval was blocked; these claims were checked through indexed official material, a weaker audit trail than a fully accessible report. [FY2025 impact report](https://rmmfi.org/2025-impact-report).

**Capital and sustainability.** Loans must be funded and losses absorbed; coaching and volunteer coordination require operating resources. Interest receipts should not be assumed to fund the whole service. Audited funding composition, outstanding loan performance and subsidy per completed entrepreneur remain diligence questions. Its published social-return figure is a valuation model, not household cash received and not an experimental effect. [Impact and financial reports](https://rmmfi.org/impact-reports-financials/); [impact summary](https://rmmfi.org/our-impact/).

**Coverage and possible contribution.** A place-based program can provide trust and intensive support that software lacks, but cannot automatically cover people outside its geography or people for whom entrepreneurship is unsuitable. Our possible role would be helping an existing operator reduce administrative burden, identify avoidable dropouts, or measure net household outcomes. The better alternative to duplicating its curriculum is a comparison of its existing workflow with a specific jointly chosen improvement. We have no RMMFI partnership, participant access or permission to collect data. Its Colorado results cannot be exported as proof for another economy.

### Comingle: voluntary income pooling, not new income production

**Identity and flows.** Comingle, Inc. is the US income-pooling project at comingle.us, not the similarly named banking products. It proposes members contributing 7% of income and receiving an equal weekly distribution from the pool. Its site currently describes a private beta. The economic mechanism is redistribution and partial sharing of income risk, not the creation of earnings. Before external donations, total member receipts cannot exceed total contributions less costs. [Comingle](https://www.comingle.us/).

The FAQ specifies a 2% operator fee on contributions plus transaction costs, US adulthood and an established online bank account, and use of Plaid/Dwolla-linked banking and identity processes. The FAQ also describes foundation support, grants and crowdfunding as startup funding; the fee model is not proof of operating self-sufficiency. Its dependence on banking and identity verification excludes some people with the greatest access barriers. Weekly payments can still face transfer delays; member-income composition and participation determine the available amount. Tax and benefit interactions need current qualified local advice, not reliance on a general FAQ. [Comingle FAQ](https://www.comingle.us/faqs).

**Reach and evidence.** The latest dated creator update located in this review, 8 September 2026, reports growth from 48 to 62 beta members. This was available in an indexed creator update, not independently verified account records. It is not a current active-member count. Promotional future membership targets are not achieved reach. No independent causal household-outcome evaluation was located in this bounded review. [Creator update 38](https://www.indiegogo.com/en/projects/conradshaw/comingle/updates/38).

**Readiness and failure points.** The ordinary banking components already exist. The harder problems are maintaining a sufficiently broad contribution base, preventing exit or selection from undermining redistribution, handling correlated income shocks and governing a trusted pool. Steward ownership is described as an intention; it should not be treated as a verified completed governance arrangement. [Comingle about page](https://www.comingle.us/about-us).

**Constructive opportunity.** Compare a voluntary pooling design with direct grants, emergency savings and existing insurance/support arrangements, including how each serves households with no stable surplus. A useful study would examine retention, net transfers after all fees and reduced shortfalls, rather than celebrate gross circulation. Our involvement is not yet shown necessary; a new competing pool would add fragmentation and operational risk before evidence of an unmet need.

### Grassroots Economics: local commitments that can mobilize unused capacity

**Identity and model.** Kenya-based Grassroots Economics Foundation has a long history of community-currency work. Its current documentation describes Cosmo-Local Credit, evolving from Sarafu. Vouchers represent commitments redeemable for goods or services, with pools facilitating exchange. This can address a coordination problem: people have useful capacity but insufficient national currency to transact. A voucher does not manufacture food, imported fuel or cash-out liquidity. [Current documentation](https://docs.grassecon.org/); [platform history](https://docs.cosmolocal.credit/introduction/history/).

**Scale must be dated.** The foundation's research page gives a historical Celo-network snapshot for July 2023–July 2025: 26,367 users, 285,197 peer-to-peer exchanges, 188 active pools and 745 vouchers. These are network metrics, not verified distinct beneficiary households or current CLC coverage. Its migration documentation explicitly cautions that historical accounts, balances and obligations did not necessarily migrate. [Research and data](https://grassrootseconomics.org/research/).

**Outcome evidence exists, but is narrower than a welfare claim.** A published randomized Sarafu transfer study reports short-run increases in activity and income measured within the community currency. The paper itself cautions against extrapolating closed-network expenditure into national-currency expenditure. That distinction matters: increased ledger activity could reflect useful exchange, substitution or accounting effects. Historical Sarafu findings do not validate all components of the current platform. [Mqamelo, Frontiers in Blockchain, 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/blockchain/articles/10.3389/fbloc.2021.739751/full). A separate public transaction dataset supports reproducibility, but transaction data alone cannot establish nutrition, household purchasing power or displacement. [Mattsson et al., Scientific Data 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-022-01539-4).

**Delivery and financial risks.** Real redemption depends on local issuers, trusted stewards and available goods. Paper approaches are possible; USSD/SMS access must be checked for the particular live deployment rather than assumed from historical Sarafu. Some wider routing and liquidity arrangements remain proposed or deployment-dependent. A published loan template makes clear that an advance can remain repayable and that transferring collateral need not extinguish the debt; it is not free income. The foundation solicits donations and directed staking rewards for programs and open-source work. Current audited funding, grant dependence, liquidity resources and actual operating cost were not established here. [Funding pathways](https://grassrootseconomics.org/get-involved). [Access FAQ](https://docs.grassecon.org/edu/faq/); [January 2026 loan template](https://docs.grassecon.org/commons/sprout-loan-terms/).

**Constructive opportunity.** Where a community already has credible unused capacity and unmet reciprocal needs, compare local clearing against a simple non-blockchain ledger and a cash-supported service. Measure goods actually delivered, redemption delays, cash displacement, participant time and distribution of gains. Do not introduce currency merely because the technology is available. No current local partner or suitable community has been established for this project.

### GoodDollar: digital distribution with real funding and redemption constraints

**Identity and model.** GoodDollar distributes G$ through a digital basic-income model backed by a reserve and external funding/yield mechanisms. Its own site says G$ is **not a stablecoin**. A reserve, transaction activity and a token balance do not ensure a predictable amount of spendable local money. As read on 23 October, its homepage displays both 800,000+ and 640,000+ onboarded, alongside 100,000 monthly active users and $6 million in transactions, without a reconciled measurement date. These are organizational counters, not verified current unique humans or household benefits. [GoodDollar](https://www.gooddollar.org/).

**Flows and delivery.** Capital or donations support reserves; investment returns and program rules support distribution. Recipients must be able to access the system, satisfy its person-verification processes, transact safely and find useful redemption or cash-out. Legacy official buy/sell documentation describes a 3% reserve exit fee with an exception, but also contains old network examples; it does not verify the fee or route available in a particular current deployment. Conversion to a crypto asset is not equivalent to cash received at a local agent. Targeted GoodCollective pools offer another distribution mechanism, but their existence does not prove recipient welfare gains. [Conversion documentation](https://docs.gooddollar.org/user-guides/buy-and-sell-gusd); [GoodCollective](https://www.gooddollar.org/goodcollective).

**A current operational stress test.** An official post-mortem dated 20 October describes a September 4 exploit affecting reserves and decentralized-exchange liquidity, about $125,000 in total, and subsequent recovery operations. It reports claims resumed September 9, reserves reopened September 29 and refunds executed October 15, while a 550-million-G$ pool remained blocked. These are the operator's account, not an independent chain audit by us. The episode demonstrates smart-contract, monitoring, liquidity and governance risks even when a service has substantial historical activity. It would be inaccurate either to ignore the incident or to describe all services as still paused. [GoodLabs incident account, October 2026](https://discourse.gooddollar.org/t/gooddollar-incident-post-mortem-september-4-attack/9164).

**Evidence and useful next comparison.** No independent causal evaluation of net household income or durable welfare was located in this review. That is a search limitation, not proof none exists. The constructive alternative to launching another token is an independent study of a real distribution route: how much usable value arrives after verification failures, fees, travel, liquidity losses and support costs, compared with regulated mobile money or cash. If the token route reaches otherwise excluded people more safely or cheaply, retain it; if not, use the simpler rail. We have not established permission, participants or a reason GoodDollar needs our software.

## 4. Technology readiness is not deployment readiness

The 2025 Global Findex is based on surveys conducted in 2024. It documents widespread account access alongside a large remaining unbanked population. Owning an account does not establish independent control, affordable cash-out, resilience or safe borrowing. [World Bank Global Findex 2025](https://www.worldbank.org/en/publication/globalfindex/report). GSMA's 2026 reporting identifies roughly 3.1 billion people living within mobile coverage but not using mobile internet. Its barriers research emphasizes affordability, skills and safety. Availability of a signal is not availability of a usable service. [GSMA September 2026 release](https://www.gsma.com/newsroom/press-release/gsma-warns-of-emerging-global-ai-divide-as-three-billion-people-remain-offline-and-smartphone-component-costs-soar/); [barriers research](https://www.gsma.com/somic/barriers-to-mobile-internet-adoption-and-use/).

| Function | Technical readiness | Deployment gap and better test |
|---|---|---|
| Identity and duplicate prevention | Established identity checks and account systems exist. | People may lack documents, share phones or fail automated checks. Compare assisted verification and appeal paths; never put sensitive identity or poverty status on a public chain. |
| Payment and cash-out | Bank transfers and mobile money are mature in many settings; crypto transfer is technically possible. | Local agent liquidity, fees, distance and account control determine spendability. Test total delivered value and failed withdrawals, not transaction speed alone. |
| Offline/low-connectivity access | Paper, assisted service, SMS and USSD are established approaches. | Availability varies by operator, language and location. Observe completion by people using basic or shared phones, not only smartphone owners. |
| AI-assisted operations | Drafting, translation, document extraction and reconciliation can support staff. | Incorrect advice, local-language errors and hidden review labor can negate savings. Compare staff-led service with AI-assisted, human-checked service on accuracy, completion and total staff time. No autonomous eligibility, debt or benefit-loss decisions. |
| Fraud and accountability | Logs, reconciliation, access controls and exception detection are usable. | Local coercion, diversion, false exclusion and grievance handling require accountable people. Measure errors in both directions and redress time; publish aggregate audit results rather than personal histories. |
| Interoperability | APIs and standard records can connect organizations. | Consent, data rights, incentives and incompatible procedures are often the binding problems. Begin with minimal exportable records and no forced new wallet. |
| Community clearing | Ledger and voucher software is available. | Redemption, dispute resolution and a viable network of goods are not software features. Compare against a manual ledger before adding a chain. |
| Regulation and governance | Existing regulated intermediaries can provide a foundation. | Credit, employment, payments, benefits, research and data obligations vary locally. Obtain qualified jurisdiction-specific review before any pilot; this report is not legal clearance. |

The main coverage opportunities are therefore **people excluded from an otherwise effective service; households missing a useful combination of support; and earning opportunities that fail because one concrete, solvable barrier remains**. A missing app feature is not itself evidence of any of these gaps.

## 5. Ranked opportunity architectures

These rankings concern our next research direction, not global estimates of returns. They combine strength of evidence, fit with the north star, plausibility of additionality and burden of delivery. None has a verified partner or recruited participant today.

| Rank and architecture | Causal path and proposed role | Strong alternative / decisive comparison | Main failure or exclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Existing-service cash-plus delivery** — non-blockchain default | Help a competent local program reach an excluded group, deliver its support reliably and resolve a documented bottleneck. Better delivery → fewer shortfalls and more sustainable livelihoods. | Existing service unchanged, and cash alone where appropriate. Compare completion, net resources and cost per sustained improvement. | We add bureaucracy; no real coverage gap; weak local capacity; people need services or protection that an enterprise program cannot supply. |
| **2. First reliable earnings bridge** | With independently evidenced buyers or employers, support a useful first task, a fair payment and portable performance evidence; remove a specific access cost without trapping the worker in debt. | Normal hiring/service plus an equivalent unrestricted grant. Measure later unsubsidized earnings, buyer repeat demand and displacement. | No buyer; one-off subsidized work only; unpaid preparation; automation erodes demand; selection favors already advantaged workers. |
| **3. Local reciprocal-capacity clearing** | An existing trusted community matches unused productive capacity to real needs, using redeemable commitments only where national-currency scarcity prevents exchange. | Simple ledger or cash-supported coordination. Measure fulfilled needs and net household value after time/cost, not token turnover. | No redeemable supply; imported essentials require cash; local power captures benefits; credit obligations accumulate. |
| **4. Voluntary risk pooling / digital distribution** | Pool contributions or distribute external support to smooth shortfalls through a suitable rail. | Direct grants, emergency savings and established payment channels. Measure net support, retention, reliability and excluded users. | Correlated shocks, thin funding, adverse selection, identity exclusion, reserve/security/cash-out risk. |

Rank 2 supplies a constructive use for the project's first-payment and microloan learning. It does not assume all poor people should sell digital work or that agents buying from agents establish a market. Rank 3 draws a useful distinction between a lack of national cash and a lack of real productive capacity. Rank 4 separates the funding problem from the distribution technology. RMMFI, Comingle, Grassroots Economics and GoodDollar are useful teachers and potential future comparison cases—not presumed partners or turnkey answers.

## 6. Proposed integrated approach: stability, opportunity, evidence

This is a **research architecture**, not an authorized launch. Its integration and targeting rules are hypotheses.

**Start with the household's constraint.** A local accountable team, with participant consent, distinguishes immediate deprivation from an earning obstacle. People unable to work retain a support/service route. Do not make aid conditional on a business plan or successful AI interaction.

**Use the least burdensome effective support.** First check existing benefits and services, then cash or an appropriate grant where support is needed. If someone has a real opportunity, establish the buyer, task, fair terms, necessary inputs and risks. Prefer buyer prepayment or a small non-repayable barrier-removal payment where justified. A loan becomes an option only when its incremental value exceeds fees, repayment risk and simpler alternatives. Loan principal is not income.

**Convert a first opportunity into a reliable next one.** Provide practical human support and, where appropriate, portable evidence of performance. The target is subsequent voluntary, unsubsidized earning or reduced necessary expenses—not a sequence of project-funded tasks. A worker-payment guarantee is only a possible later instrument if a real counterparty problem warrants it and legal, capital and payment approvals exist. Existing contract code is neither evidence of demand nor permission to fund such a guarantee.

**Keep enough protection to survive shocks.** An affordable savings mechanism, access to existing support, and a reliable grievance path can prevent a short setback from destroying productive progress. Community exchange is optional and requires redeemability; tokens are not the default household asset.

**Measure the marginal contribution.** Compare the combined approach with what the household would otherwise receive. Separate a transfer's immediate purchasing power from future earnings, and measure both without treating leisure, care or improved health as failure. Look for sustained gains, not just completion while subsidies continue.

The architecture would be disconfirmed if the claimed obstacle is rare, the existing service already resolves it, participants prefer the baseline, buyers do not return without subsidies, gains disappear after costs, harms or exclusion rise, or cash alone performs as well at lower cost. The alternative would then be the simpler baseline, another qualified mechanism, or no project intervention—not another renamed pilot.

## 7. Research-to-pilot gates, resources and measurement

No recruitment, partner inquiry, spending, account creation, private-data collection or launch was performed for this report.

| Gate | Owned deliverable and evidence required | Decision, effort and timing |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Strategic research** | Codex owns this synthesis; Claude supplies an adversarial written challenge with better alternatives and comparison tests. | Complete this report and preserve dissent. Existing public evidence supports a direction, not a pilot. |
| **1. Choose one context** | Codex produces a comparison of candidate populations and existing services; a qualified local practitioner must validate feasibility before selection. | Proposed 2–4-week diagnostic scope after a context/resource decision, not an automatic timetable or commission. Prefer documented exclusion and a capable service over an easy-to-reach online audience. |
| **2. Establish additionality and permissions** | With separately authorized access, local staff and opt-in beneficiaries verify the actual obstacle, current baseline, delivery capacity and acceptable outcomes. | Stop if no important gap or no responsible delivery organization. No operator introduction is required; any eventual outreach must have its own applicable authority. |
| **3. Design a safe operational test** | Named delivery lead, independent evaluator, local legal/data review, consent and grievance procedures, costed protocol and comparison design. | Obtain actual quotes, participant burden estimates, payment responsibilities and explicit funding approval. A small feasibility sample tests delivery and safety, not poverty impact. |
| **4. Comparative pilot** | Evaluate existing service/cash baseline against the specific addition. Use random allocation when appropriate and ethical; otherwise state the limitations of the comparison. | Sample size follows variance, minimum meaningful effect and power analysis—not an arbitrary five-person impact claim. Predefine follow-up, ideally covering income seasonality and at least 6–12 months for persistence. |
| **5. Continue, simplify or stop** | Independent outcome assessment, cost accounting, participant feedback, harms, spillovers and exclusion review. | Scale only if additional net benefit justifies full cost and delivery can be sustained. Prefer the simpler baseline if performance is equivalent. |

A realistic budget must include participant support or productive inputs, local staff, translation/access assistance, safeguarding, payment fees and liquidity, evaluation, legal/data work, complaints, losses and contingency. Loan capital is a balance-sheet requirement distinct from operating expense; defaults and collection costs are real. AI time being cheap does not make local delivery free.

No credible total budget can be quoted before context, delivery partner, sample and intervention are specified. Published program costs are useful reference classes, not procurement quotes. The economic-inclusion literature shows substantial variation in package composition and setting; a single borrowed unit cost would create false precision. [World Bank report and cost discussion](https://www.peiglobal.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/The%20State%20of%20Economic%20Inclusion%20Report%202024_0.pdf).

**Outcome accounting.** Track earnings net of business inputs, fees, travel, additional care expenses and debt costs; transfers separately; necessary-expense reductions; and time burden. Do not count borrowed principal, inter-wallet transfers or gross token value as income. Measure results relative to the baseline, include failures and dropouts, and report who was excluded. Add consumption security, food insecurity, debt distress and participant-defined wellbeing. Longer-term durability requires follow-up after support ends. Local inflation and displacement of other workers or sellers can reduce net social gains even when treated participants improve.

Our own contribution must also be tested: does assistance from this project improve delivery or outcomes enough to justify coordination and oversight? If a capable organization can achieve the same result without us, the constructive choice is to make our research reusable and avoid becoming another required intermediary.
